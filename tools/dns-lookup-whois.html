What This Tool Does

Most DNS and WHOIS checkers show raw data across separate pages, and you still have to work out what it means. I built this tool to put everything about a domain on one screen: its DNS records, its registration details (registrar, age, expiry, status) and its email security. Every item comes with a plain-English explanation and a fix when something is wrong. I use it for site migrations, domain due diligence, and for checking that a brand can't be spoofed by email.

Key Features

📋
All DNS Records
A, AAAA, CNAME, MX, NS, TXT, CAA, SOA and DNSSEC. Each record type can be filtered and comes with a one-line explanation.
📇
Registration via RDAP
Registrar, age, expiry countdown and status codes explained. Data comes from RDAP, the official replacement for WHOIS, with a WHOIS fallback.
✉️
Email Trust Check
SPF (including the 10-lookup limit), DMARC policy, DKIM auto-detection and BIMI. Shows whether someone can send email pretending to be you.
🩺
Health Score + Fixes
Flags an expiring domain, a missing transfer lock, broken www or non-www, weak SPF/DMARC, no CAA and no DNSSEC, each with a clear fix.
🏷️
Host, CDN & Email Provider
Detects who hosts the site (Cloudflare, AWS, Akamai…), who runs its DNS, and which email service it uses.
✅
Verified Services + CSV
Shows Search Console, Bing, Meta and other verification records, then downloads everything as one CSV report.

How to Use It

1
Enter a domain
Type any domain, or paste a full URL. The tool works out the main domain for you.
2
Read the health score and fixes
Start with the Issues & Fixes list, then look through the registration, DNS records and email trust sections.
3
Fix, re-check, export
Make the changes at your DNS provider or registrar, run the check again, and download the CSV for your records.

Why I Recommend It

In finance, a domain is a trust signal. An expired domain, an unlocked transfer or a missing DMARC record can lead to lost traffic or phishing emails sent in your brand's name. This tool catches those risks in seconds and explains them clearly enough to hand straight to a developer or IT team.

  • Uses RDAP, which ICANN made the official WHOIS replacement in 2025
  • Reads DNS live through Google and Cloudflare public resolvers
  • Checks email authentication against the Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules

Note: owner names and emails are hidden for most domains under GDPR / privacy protection, so the tool shows "Redacted for privacy" rather than guessing.
