What This Tool Does

Give it a page URL, a primary keyword, or both. It locks onto one root keyword — either the one you type in, or the strongest phrase it finds on the page — and expands it the way real keyword tools do: running the root through Google's own Autocomplete across every letter of the alphabet plus common question words, and layering in prefix and suffix modifiers on top. Anything that drifts off-topic gets filtered out automatically. The result is a proper keyword map, not just a word-frequency list: root, secondary, prefix, suffix and long-tail keywords, each scored and tagged.

How to Use It

1
Enter a URL, a primary keyword, or both
A primary keyword sets the root directly; a URL lets the tool boost anything that's already on the page. Giving both works best.
2
Let it sweep and expand
The root gets run through a full Google Autocomplete sweep plus prefix/suffix modifiers, and off-topic results are filtered out.
3
Browse by category and export
Flip between Root, Secondary, Prefix, Suffix and Long-tail tabs, then export the full list to CSV for your content plan.
Worth knowing: this tool ranks keywords by relevance — how strongly a phrase relates to the root and how Google Autocomplete responds to it — not by verified search volume or CPC. There's no paid keyword database behind it, so treat the list as a fast way to map out keyword opportunities and content angles, not exact monthly search numbers.

Why I Built It

Before writing or refreshing a BankBazaar page, I want a proper keyword map for the topic — not just a list of words pulled off the page, but the prefix, suffix and long-tail variations people actually search.
