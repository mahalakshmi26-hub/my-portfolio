Title Tags 101: How to Write Titles That Rank and Get Clicks

M
Mahalakshmi Marimuthu
·5 min read·July 2026

Your title tag does two jobs at once: it tells search engines what your page is about, and it convinces a real person scrolling through results to actually click it. Most beginners only optimize for the first job. Here's how to nail both.

What is a Title Tag?

The title tag is the clickable headline that appears in search results and browser tabs. It's set in your page's HTML like this:

<title>Your Page Title Here</title>

It's one of the first things both Google and a human see about your page — which is exactly why it deserves more thought than it usually gets.

The Two Jobs a Title Tag Has to Do

  • Relevance for search engines: including the right keyword helps Google understand what the page is about and match it to relevant searches.
  • Click-through appeal for humans: ranking well doesn't matter if nobody clicks. The title needs to feel worth clicking compared to the other 9 results on the page.
Simple way to think about it: the keyword gets you the ranking opportunity. The wording around it gets you the click.

Basic Rules to Follow

1. Keep it under ~60 characters

Google typically truncates titles beyond this length in search results, cutting off with "..." Aim to fit your full message before that point.

2. Put the primary keyword near the front

Search engines tend to give slightly more weight to words that appear earlier in the title, and users scanning results also read left to right first.

3. Make every title unique

Duplicate title tags across multiple pages confuse both search engines and users about which page is which. Every page should have its own distinct title.

4. Avoid keyword stuffing

Weak
Running Shoes | Buy Running Shoes | Best Running Shoes Online | Cheap Running Shoes
Better
Best Running Shoes for Beginners (2026 Buying Guide)

The second version reads naturally, still contains the keyword, and gives a real reason to click — the promise of a helpful, current guide.

Writing Titles People Actually Want to Click

TechniqueWhy It Works
Numbers ("7 Ways to...")Sets a clear, scannable expectation for the content's structure
Specificity ("for Beginners", "for Small Teams")Signals the content is relevant to the exact searcher, not generic
Freshness ("2026 Guide")Suggests up-to-date, currently relevant information
Clear benefit or outcomeAnswers "what's in it for me" before the click even happens
Avoid clickbait. A title that promises something the page doesn't deliver increases bounce rate — people leave immediately, which is itself a negative signal to search engines over time.

Title Tag vs. H1: Are They the Same?

Not necessarily. The title tag is what shows in search results; the H1 is the main visible heading on the page itself. They can be identical, but they don't have to be — the title tag can be written slightly more for search and click appeal, while the H1 can be written more for on-page clarity.

The takeaway: a good title tag is short, specific, keyword-forward, and honest about what the page delivers. Write it for the person scanning ten blue links, not just for the algorithm reading your HTML.
